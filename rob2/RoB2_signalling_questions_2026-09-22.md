**D5 Selection of the reported result: changed from "Some concerns" to "Low"**

| Q | Answer | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1 Analysed per a pre-specified plan finalised before unblinded data were available | **Y** | Body composition was pre-specified, not post hoc: "Body composition (total fat, total lean body mass, and regional [abdominal] visceral fat mass) was measured in the DXA subpopulation **as a supportive secondary end point**" (Wilding 2021, Methods, End Points). The analysis follows the pre-specified treatment policy estimand described in the protocol and statistical analysis plan. |
| 5.2 Selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the domain | **PN** | Total lean body mass at week 68 is reported once, in both kilograms and percent, in Table S5. There is no indication of several eligible lean-mass measurements from which one was chosen. |
| 5.3 Selected from multiple eligible analyses | **PN** | Table S5 reports the treatment policy estimand; the trial product estimand is reported separately in Table S2 for other endpoints. Both estimands were pre-specified, so reporting the pre-specified primary estimand is not selection. |
| **D5 judgement** | **Low** | |

**Why the previous judgement changed.** In the original submission we rated D5 as "some concerns" because the body-composition endpoint carried no multiplicity adjustment and no p-value. We accept the reviewer's point that this is not a domain 5 criterion. Domain 5 asks whether *this particular numerical result* is likely to have been selected, after seeing the data, from several eligible measurements or analyses. It was not: the endpoint is named prospectively in the trial report as a supportive secondary endpoint, one lean-mass value at one time point is reported, and it is analysed under the pre-specified primary estimand. The authors' own statement that "supportive secondary end-point analyses were not controlled for multiple comparisons and should not be used to infer definitive treatment effects" is a caution about the strength of inference, which we handle in the certainty assessment (GRADE imprecision), not in risk of bias.

A second reason for the change is internal consistency. SURMOUNT-1 and SUSTAIN-8 are in the same position: in SUSTAIN-8, lean mass is an "additional efficacy endpoint" and only fat mass was confirmatory, and in SURMOUNT-1 there was likewise no multiplicity adjustment. Had we kept "some concerns" for STEP-1 on that ground, we would have had to apply it to those trials too. The consistent and correct answer is that none of the three meets the domain 5 criteria.

**Overall: Low** (previously "Some concerns").
